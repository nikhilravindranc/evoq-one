# ServiceOps v2 home page: content deck for approval (your seven section flow)

Route: `/products/serviceops-v2` (noindex, v1 stays). Nothing is built yet. This is the copy, the pictures each screen needs, and the rules the build will follow. Please mark what to change.

The home page is the story. The Features page keeps every detail, so each screen ends with one quiet link into the matching part of Features.

Sources for every claim: the ServiceOps features list, the help guide, and the AI hub in the demo app. Where a feature is partial, demo or planned, the line says so.

---

## 0. Voice and look

**Language we avoid** (the habits that read as machine made on Zuper and Fieldproxy)
- A pill above the headline, and a caps eyebrow over every section.
- "Not X, but Y" headlines, slogans built in threes, a coloured last line in every headline, a row of ticks under the button.
- Stat strips, "trusted by" rows and quotes. We have no real numbers or customers to show.
- Words such as intelligent, seamless, powerful, effortless, all in one, operating system, unlock, streamline.
- Dashes in copy.

**What we do**
- Plain sentences that name real things in the product, one idea a screen, shown with a real screen of the app.
- A few handwritten margin notes (Caveat) pointing at a detail, as Zoho does. Listed at the end.
- Honest labels: Partial, Planned, Demo AI.

**Look**
- White and light grey surfaces. Amber only in the main areas: one full width band, the buttons, small marks.
- Real photographs in arch and rounded frames, with at most two floating UI cards on top.
- Dotted grid on the grey bands, and hand-drawn dashed lines.

### 3. The service journey (amber band)
**Headline**
Every kind of job has its own path.

**Text**
Installation, Service, Repair, Maintenance and Inspection are different work. Each work type carries its own stages, questions and conditions, set by your managers, so a job follows the right path from request to completion.

**Work type picker** (a photo for each, and its stages)
- **Installation.** Coordinate new equipment installation, required checks and completion. *Requested, Approved, Checks, Installed, Complete.*
- **Service.** Handle customer reported service needs and planned service visits. *Requested, Scheduled, Visit, Complete.*
- **Repair.** Record the issue, guide the repair process and document the work performed. *Issue recorded, Approved, Repair, Documented, Complete.*
- **Maintenance.** Schedule recurring maintenance and raise work orders when service is due. *Plan due, Work order, Visit, Complete.*
- **Inspection.** Follow inspection stages and record the required findings. *Scheduled, Inspect, Findings recorded, Complete.*

**Below the picker, the five checkpoints every job passes**
- **Request.** A call, an email, the web form, online booking, the customer portal or a technician. One inbox.
- **Approval.** The coordinator turns it into a work order and an estimate. The customer approves from a private link.
- **Dispatch.** Ranked technicians, each with a reason. Nothing is booked until you apply it.
- **Visit.** Checklist, photos, parts and a signature. The screens keep working without signal.
- **Invoice.** Ready to invoice, a private page to pay, and a short page to rate the visit.

**Link:** See every feature in the order a job moves (Features, Work)
**Handwritten note:** same record all the way through

### 4. Office and field coordination
**Headline**
How the day runs, from the dispatch board to the job site.

**Text**
The dispatcher fills the day. The technician works it. The manager checks how it went. All three are looking at the same jobs.

**Dispatcher** (first thing in the morning)
Sees the queue, the day on a timeline and the technicians on a map. Pick a work order and ServiceOps ranks the technicians for it and says why in plain words. Optimize the day proposes a full set of assignments and lists what it held back. You apply it, or you don't.
*Screen:* Dispatch with the ranked options card.

**Technician** (on site)
Opens today's jobs on a phone with directions and a call button. Steps marked required cannot be skipped. Photos, parts used and notes go on the job, and the customer signs. If the signal drops, the screens keep working and sync later.
*Screen:* Technician job on a phone.

**Service manager** (end of the day)
Sees the visits that could not be finished and the work waiting for approval, and reads the weekly report. Insights and Profitability show what each job earned.
*Screen:* Insights or Profitability.

**Below the three blocks, one quiet line**
Service coordinators and administrators have their own screens too, and an administrator decides who sees what. See the five roles.

**Link:** See what each role can open (Features, Roles)
**Handwritten notes:** ranked, with the reason · required steps can't be skipped · works offline

### 5. Beyond the service visit
**Headline**
Customers, equipment and money carry on after the visit.

**Customers**
A booking page for new requests. A private link to approve or decline an estimate. A private page to view and pay an invoice. A short page to rate the visit. None of it needs an account.
*Picture:* a customer with a phone, and the request and estimate pages.

**Equipment**
Every piece of equipment has a record with make, model, serial number, warranty and status, plus its history and its next service. Gold and Silver service plans show maintenance due in the next 30, 60 or 90 days and raise the work orders.
*Picture:* a nameplate close-up, and an asset record.

**Money**
Book labour, parts and other costs on a job and ServiceOps shows its margin. Profitability splits margin by job, customer, trade, work type, technician and service plan. Billing counts what is ready to invoice, what is out and what is overdue. Some payment and accounting tasks sit in the linked EVOQ Billing product.
*Picture:* a Costing tab, and Profitability by work type.

**Link:** See customers, assets and money in detail (Features)
**Handwritten notes:** no account needed · next service, 30, 60 or 90 days out

### 6. Systems and AI assistance
**Headline**
Connected to your systems, with AI where it helps.

**Your systems**
Where supported integrations are available, service information can be shared with the systems you already run: accounting and financial applications, CRM and customer communication systems, email and messaging services, payment providers, and ERP and inventory systems. ServiceOps can also work alongside CRM, Desk, Inventory, Billing and Sync from EVOQ. No partner logos until each integration is confirmed.

**EVI and the agents**
EVI is the assistant you open from any screen. Ask what needs attention today and it answers from your records, most urgent first, and shows only what your role may open. The agents each watch one area and list what needs a person. In ServiceOps they draft and flag. A person sends, books or decides. Until you connect a model, answers come from a built-in demo model that is clearly labelled.

**Agent explorer** (a face for each, one card at a time: what it does, what it reads, what it changes, its guardrail)
Intake Agent · Dispatch Agent · Quote Follow-up Agent · Collections Agent · Risk Flags Agent · Coaching Agent. The card text is the same as in the app's AI hub, for example: "Dispatch Agent. Finds work orders that are ready to schedule, and technicians who are booked past a full day or free while work waits. Reads work orders, jobs and technician schedules. Changes nothing. Suggests, never books."

**Left out on purpose:** phone and SMS receptionist, remote video expert, review import and posting, and agents on a daily timer. They are planned. The Features page lists them under "Not available yet".

**Link:** See all the AI helpers and agents (Features, AI)
**Handwritten note:** drafts and flags, a person decides
