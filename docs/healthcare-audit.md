# Healthcare solutions: page audit

Pages audited: `/solutions/healthcare`, Patient Relationships (and its features page), Practice Management, Patient Engagement, and the Patient Access & Payments block. Read as the people who buy: a clinic or practice manager, an aesthetics or wellness owner, a dental practice manager, a hospital or network administrator, and a diagnostic centre operations lead. Findings come from the rendered pages at 1440px. I did not test phone width, and the section and word counts are approximate.

## 1. Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Are the solutions clear to the buyer? | **Partly.** The three solution names are clear. The fourth, Patient Access & Payments, has no page, and Booking and Billing are filed in three different places. |
| Is the main page overcrowded? | **Not by word count (about 1,170 words), but by repetition.** The same seven products are introduced through four different lenses, so a visitor decodes the product map four times. |
| Do the sub pages help? | **Unevenly.** Patient Engagement is tight. Practice Management has one overloaded section. Patient Relationships repeats one idea across eight sections and is too long. |

## 3. How the buyer experiences the main page

1. **Hero.** "Stronger patient relationships. More efficient practice operations." could sit on any healthcare software page, so it fails the competitor test. The body does the real work ("Track referrals and enquiries through to the first appointment…"). The six floating hero chips (Enquiries, Appointments, Providers, Billing, Patient engagement, Better outcomes) mix real modules with a claim ("Better outcomes"), and each carries a filler line such as "Schedule with ease" and "Build lasting relationships".
2. **"What would you like to improve?"** A good idea, because it starts from the buyer's goal. Weak execution: "Revenue Optimization" is jargon, and the four "Explore" links go to four different kinds of place. Three scroll down the same page (`#healthcare-crm`, `#practice-management`, `#patient-journey`) and one opens a page (Patient Engagement).
3. **"From first enquiry to ongoing care."** A six-stage journey (Capture to Re-engage). It re-describes the products by stage.
4. **"Patient relationships, practice operations and engagement in one place."** The solutions section: three cards, then Patient Access & Payments.
5. **"Built for clinics, dental, aesthetics, hospitals and diagnostics."** Six care settings. The hero already names four of the same groups.
6. **AI, standards, integrations, closing.** Four more sections.

**The four lenses.** Outcomes (4), journey stages (6), solutions (3 plus 1), care settings (6). A visitor has to decide which lens to use, and each one lists the same CRM, Practice Management, Campaigns, Surveys, Loyalty, Booking and Billing.

**Where each buyer gets stuck**
- **Practice manager (dental, clinic):** wants to know how the day runs. The hero leads with "relationships", and the answer is two clicks away on the Practice Management page.
- **Aesthetics and wellness owner:** the page fits well. Enquiry to consultation to package to return visit is covered.
- **Hospital or network administrator:** wants multi-location, referrals, standards and privacy. These come late (section 7 of 9) and the main page never says whether EVOQ replaces the clinical record.
- **Diagnostic centre lead:** one card and nothing more. There is no diagnostics-specific solution.

## 4. Solutions clarity

| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Patient Access & Payments is an orphan | It appears only inside the solutions section. It has no link and no page. The Patient Engagement page lists it without a link. |
| Booking and Billing are filed in three places | Main page: under Patient Access & Payments. Practice Management page: under Patient Engagement ("Extend the experience with Booking Engine, Billing, Campaigns…"). Outcome cards: as "Appointment Booking" chips. |
| One product, three names | "Booking Engine", "Appointment Booking" and "EVOQ Booking". The site now has EVOQ Bookings with an Appointments product (provider schedules, availability, reminders, deposits, waitlists). The healthcare pages should use that name. Confirm. |
| Inconsistent card footers | "Powered by EVOQ CRM" next to "Powered by Practice Management". |
| The record boundary is not stated | The Patient Relationships page says "without turning your CRM into a clinical records system". The main page and Practice Management say "record visit details, notes, forms and documents" without saying what is and is not a clinical record. Buyers will ask. |
| Care settings differ by page | Main page: six. Practice Management: seven (adds dermatology and physiotherapy, drops hospitals). Patient Relationships: its own list. |

## 5. Sub pages

**Patient Relationships (the longest).** Eight sections state the same idea in different words: one connected workspace, shared context, follow ups, visibility. For example "Every patient relationship starts somewhere", "From enquiry to ongoing care", "Give every team member the context", "Track. Follow up. Build relationships." and "Stay connected. At the right time." Roughly half of them can merge. The features page H1 is "Everything your team needs to manage patient relationships."

**Practice Management.** The second section holds two topics (patient information and front desk operations) in about 2,700px and 416 words, which is the most crowded block in the set. Seven practice-type cards repeat the same sentence shape. The closing "Start with scheduling and add more as the practice grows" is the clearest line on the page.

**Patient Engagement (the best of the three).** One section per product: Campaigns, Surveys and Loyalty, each with use cases. The weakness is hedging: "where supported", "where available" and "eligible returning patients, where supported" appear repeatedly, especially for Loyalty. If a feature exists, say what it does. If it does not, leave it out.

## 6. Copy rule violations to fix

Absolutes and filler on the main page and sub pages: "Schedule with ease", "Make appointments and payments easier to manage", "Better outcomes", "Build lasting relationships", "Connect the tools your team already uses", "same view of each patient", "One patient history across every team", "every touchpoint", "each stage", "Every enquiry", "Every patient relationship", "Everything your team needs". Also "Support key steps…", "Reduce routine work" and the hedged "where supported" lines. The H2 "Privacy, security and integration questions to settle before you adopt" is honest but reads as a warning. The standards lists name HIPAA, DPDP, UAE PDPL, ABDM, NABIDH, Riayati and Malaffi. Product and legal should confirm each acronym is a claim EVOQ can make.

## 7. Recommendations

| Priority | Change |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Rewrite the hero** in the family pattern, for example "Enquiries followed up, appointments organized, patients kept in touch." Drop the filler chip lines. |
| 2 | **Replace the four lenses with one structure**, the model used on the Field Service and Construction pages: side tabs by care setting (clinics and specialty practices, dental, aesthetics and wellness, hospitals and networks, diagnostics), a staged scene each, and numbered solution rows. That takes the page from nine sections to about six. Keep "What would you like to improve?" as a short entry row. |
| 3 | **Decide the product map.** Either four solutions with a real page for Patient Access & Payments, or three with Booking and Billing inside Practice Management. Use one name for each product. Align the booking name with EVOQ Bookings. |
| 4 | **State the record boundary** once, near the top: what EVOQ holds and what stays in your clinical system. |
| 5 | **Bring standards and integrations forward** for hospital and network buyers, and name them plainly. |
| 6 | **Cut Patient Relationships from sixteen sections to about eight.** Split the overloaded Practice Management section in two. Strip the hedges from Patient Engagement. |
| 7 | **Fix the copy list in section 6.** Make the outcome cards link consistently. |
| 8 | **Give diagnostics its own solution row** (referrals, enquiries, patient communication) or say plainly that it is covered by Patient Relationships. |

## 8. Facts to confirm before a rewrite

1. Is "Booking Engine" the same product as EVOQ Bookings (Appointments)?
2. Does Patient Access & Payments get a page, or fold into Practice Management?
3. What does Loyalty actually do today (rewards, memberships, tiers)?
4. Which compliance and standards claims can be stated as they stand?
5. What does EVOQ store for a visit (notes, forms, documents), and where is the clinical record boundary?
6. Which integrations in the integrations section are live today?
